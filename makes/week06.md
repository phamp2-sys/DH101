## Reflection
When machines “read” literature, we gain new ways of noticing patterns, but we also lose
some of the uncertainty and personal experience that comes with human reading.
On the one hand, Voyant helped me see the speech as a collection of repeated words and
patterns. For example, words such as “woman,” “man,” “ain’t,” and “rights” appeared
frequently, showing how repetition creates emphasis. However, Voyant could not explain
why these words mattered or connect them to Sojourner Truth’s larger argument.
On the other hand, ChatGPT approached the text differently. Instead of focusing mainly on
word patterns, it interpreted the speech through its historical context, symbolism, and literal
versus figurative meaning. This helped me see connections between Truth’s personal
experiences and the broader struggle for racial and gender equality.
The two tools also imagine “meaning” differently. Voyant treats meaning as something that
can be observed through patterns in the text, while ChatGPT claims that it can be interpreted
through context and ideas. However, ChatGPT’s interpretation can sound very confident
even when it does not clearly show which lines led to its conclusions, and based on what
argument. Voyant is more transparent about its evidence, but it cannot move as easily from
patterns to interpretation. Using both made me realize that machine reading does not simply
give us meaning, but it changes what we notice about a text depending on the method being
used.
